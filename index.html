Wave power generator

Waves lift and drop a floating ball. A steel cable drags a magnet in and out of a coil on the sea floor, and the changing magnetic field induces an alternating potential difference that is sent to shore.

How it works

  1. Waves make the floating ball rise and fall.
  2. The steel cable pulls the magnet up as the ball rises; the magnet falls back under its own weight as the ball drops.
  3. As the magnet moves in and out of the coil, the magnetic field through the coil changes. This induces a potential difference across the coil: the generator effect.
  4. The coil is part of a complete circuit through the cable to shore, so a current flows.
  5. The magnet changes direction every half wave, so the p.d. and current reverse: this is alternating current.

Things to try

Pause the simulation at the very top of a wave. The p.d. is zero because the magnet has stopped for an instant, so the field through the coil is not changing.

Double the number of turns. The peak p.d. doubles. A stronger magnet has the same effect.

Make the waves bigger or the period shorter. The magnet moves faster, so the p.d. is bigger. A shorter period also raises the frequency of the a.c.

Set the wave height to zero. No movement means no induced p.d.

Energy transfers

Kinetic energy of the waves→Kinetic energy of the ball and magnet→Electrical work done by the current→Light and thermal energy in the bulb

Higher tier: the induced current makes the coil a magnet too. Its field always opposes the motion that caused it, so the moving magnet is pushed back whichever way it moves. The waves have to do work against that force, and this is where the electrical energy comes from.

Wave power is renewable and uses no fuel, but it produces nothing when the sea is calm, and devices are costly to install and maintain at sea.

Check your understanding

Why is a potential difference induced across the coil?
The magnet moves relative to the coil, so the magnetic field passing through the coil changes (the wire cuts the magnetic field lines). A changing field through a coil induces a p.d.
Why is the current produced alternating rather than direct?
The magnet moves up, then down. When the direction of motion reverses, the direction of the induced p.d. reverses too, so the current changes direction every half wave.
Give three ways to increase the induced p.d.
Use more turns on the coil; use a stronger magnet; make the magnet move faster (bigger waves or waves with a shorter period).
The wave period is 8 s. What is the frequency of the alternating current?
f = 1 / T = 1 / 8 = 0.125 Hz. Compare that with the 50 Hz of UK mains electricity.
Look at the graphs. When is the induced p.d. largest?
When the ball passes through calm sea level, because the ball and magnet are moving fastest there. The p.d. is zero at the crest and the trough, where the magnet stops for an instant.
(Higher) Why does it get harder to move the magnet when a current flows?
The current in the coil creates its own magnetic field, which opposes the change that produced it. It exerts a force on the magnet that opposes its motion, so work must be done to keep it moving. That work becomes electrical energy.
